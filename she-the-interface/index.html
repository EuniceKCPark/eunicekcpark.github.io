She, the Interface

A virtual exhibition on the feminization of Korean AI interfaces
Curated by Eunice Park, 2026

Digital assistants feel familiar because they are built from familiar social scripts. This exhibition traces one of those scripts, the female persona as the default face of AI, from the first chatbot to generative influencers and humanoid robots, and asks visitors to notice how often they accept the default without looking.

Entrance

The default button

The exhibition turns its argument into an interaction before any image is shown. A kiosk places a large "Issue ticket" button at the center of the screen and a quieter link at the bottom offering alternative guide personas. Visitors who press the obvious button receive a ticket from Iruda telling them to look again at the bottom of the screen. Those who found the alternative are thanked instead.

The point is felt rather than stated: a design default is a choice someone else made, and accepting it is also a choice.

Room 1

She Was Always the Default

Iruda, a Korean chatbot marketed in 2020 as a 20-year-old "AI friend", reached hundreds of thousands of users in two weeks and became a target of organized sexual harassment within weeks. The room reads her promotional images through Goffman's analysis of gender display: the tilted head, the indirect gaze, the warm youthful palette.

It then sets her redesign beside the original. Iruda 2.0 returned with a cooler palette, more revealing clothing and a reshaped body. The update refined the aesthetic instead of questioning the persona. A timeline from ELIZA to Tay to Iruda shows that the pattern predates visual embodiment altogether.

Room 2

The Default, Now on Display

Hyper-realistic virtual models moved the same persona into celebrity space. The room compares three strategies. Rozy's campaign posts make the cause a backdrop to her body. Reah Keem's selfie aesthetic manufactures pseudo-intimacy through close-ups and mundane settings. Lil Miquela wears activism as styling.

Different tactics, one premise: femininity as a means. Only the object on display changes.

Room 3

She Comes on Command

Visitors are surrounded by profile screens of AI-generated influencer accounts from Korea and abroad. Corporate virtual models still performed a life; these accounts drop the pretense entirely and link straight to paid adult content. With generative AI, the user no longer watches a curated persona but produces one on demand, privately and without friction.

Room 4

She Is No Longer Pixels

The final room follows the desire past the screen: from Roxxxy, sold in 2010 as a humanoid "companion with a personality", to the 2023 Korean film JUNG_E, where a woman's consciousness is copied onto an assembly line of identical synthetic heads. Fiction here is not evidence. It names where the logic of the earlier rooms is already heading.

You have seen the default.
Will you choose it again?

From essay to space

  1. 1

    Research and visual analysis

    Close readings of 29 images spanning 1966 to 2026, grounded in Goffman, Mulvey, and work on parasocial intimacy and postfeminist selfies.

  2. 2

    Curatorial writing

    An essay written as wall text, revised across several rounds of peer feedback so each room ends on a question that opens the next.

  3. 3

    Spatial design

    Four rooms built in Metasteps with a guided path of 25 stops, ordered chronologically so the gaze escalates as the visitor walks.

  4. 4

    Interaction design

    A storyboarded kiosk flow that turns the thesis into a choice the visitor makes with their own hand, built here as a working prototype.

What it led to

Designing the entrance kiosk taught me something the essay alone could not. Most people press the button a system places in front of them. The alternative is there, but few look for it.

The same pattern runs through my research on generative AI in university classrooms, where students accepted AI outputs they could have questioned. If defaults steer people this reliably, the design question turns around: how might we design systems whose defaults nudge people toward critical thinking rather than away from it?
